# Master's Thesis: MVDR Multi-beam Azimuth-Time-Doppler Three-Dimensional Deconvolution and Filtering with Algorithms [Attached MATLAB Code]

Abstract
Active sonar is interested in the target parameters of horizontal azimuth in space-plane wave processing. For spatial domain processing, to achieve high reliability and high resolution for target parameter estimation, wavefront design is required to concentrate the array response function. The wave equation indicates that space-time has a duality. In time domain processing, the parameters of interest for active sonar are target distance and radial velocity, which cannot be directly observed and must be estimated through corresponding delay and Doppler parameters. To achieve high reliability and high resolution for target parameter estimation in time domain processing, waveform design is necessary to concentrate the ambiguity function in both delay-Doppler dimensions. A one-dimensional time signal is described by a delay-Doppler two-dimensional function (ambiguity function), which essentially is a matched filter.
Detection and estimation usually occur together. In the presence of Gaussian noise, the best processors for both are the data and copy-related matched filters. Time matching includes delay-matching filter and delay-Doppler matching filter, while spatial beamforming includes plane wave beamforming and matched field beamforming in a waveguide environment. This paper follows the decomposability of space-time under narrowband conditions, first conducting spatial processing before time processing to obtain space-time related results. Spatial beamforming converts the array element domain into the beam domain, and this paper adopts a continuous observation multibeam system. Minimum variance undistorted (MVDR) beamforming can suppress interference to improve spatial resolution. Based on the duality of space-time, this paper proposes MVDR azimuth-delay-Doppler three-dimensional interference suppression in the beam domain to enhance three-dimensional resolution.
The estimation of target parameters from measurement signals is an inverse problem solution, where matching is used for forward fitting to solve the inverse problem, and deconvolution is used for reverse solving the inverse problem. The proposed deconvolution algorithm in this paper is based on the Richardson-Lucy (R-L) iterative deconvolution algorithm with the minimum discriminant criterion. The two-dimensional matching between the measured signal after spatial beamforming and the transmitted signal is a fuzzy time-delay-Doppler two-dimensional function (sampling mutually ambiguous function), which can be modeled as a two-dimensional convolution of the self-ambiguous function of the transmitted signal and the target reflection density function. By using the R-L algorithm to perform two-dimensional deconvolution on this expectation, the time-delay-Doppler ambiguity caused by the transmitted signal is eliminated, and the target reflection density function is reconstructed, thereby obtaining high-resolution multi-target distance and radial velocity parameter estimates.
The three-dimensional MVDR interference suppression combined with R-L iterative deconvolution filtering forms the MVDR multibeam azimuth-time delay-Doppler three-dimensional deconvolution filtering system in this paper. Simulation and experimental results have verified the effectiveness of this three-dimensional processing system.

The research objective of this paper is to detect and estimate high-reliable azimuth-time delay-Doppler three-dimensional parameters for multiple moving targets underwater, and to achieve this goal, the research approach is based on the narrowband spatio-temporal factorization, first conducting spatial processing followed by time processing. Starting from the likelihood ratio detection, relevant processors are derived according to the related processors. The spatial processing and time processing are studied separately. For the high-reliable resolution of multiple targets, reliable resolution can be achieved through waveform design and wavefront design respectively, and improved spatio-temporal resolution can be achieved through MVDR interference suppression and two-dimensional deconvolution of time delay-Doppler. Therefore, the specific structure and distribution of the article are as follows:
Chapter 1 is the introduction section, focusing on the problem statement and introducing the current state of research both domestically and internationally. The article's research approach and content are also discussed. The formulation of a problem is the starting point for an article; "How can one know the clearness of a river? Because it has flowing water from its source," accurately identifying the problem can lead to the development of solutions, thereby broadening perspectives. As stated in Zheng Xuan's "Poem Catalogue Preface" from the Han Dynasty, "With one main thread, all threads are stretched out; with one piece of text, many pieces become clear." This illustrates the importance of having a clear framework.
Chapter 2 is on spatial processing, which mainly includes three parts: wavefront design, planar wave beamforming, and matched field beamforming. Wavefront design measures by the array response function to provide high-reliability spatial resolution in platform design. Planar wave beamforming includes conventional beamforming and MVDR beamforming, while matched field beamforming essentially extends from planar wave beamforming. Both are spatial matching filters. The difference lies in the application of channels, which are respectively the far-field free-space channel model and the three-dimensional waveguide channel model.
Chapter 3 is time processing, which mainly includes waveform design, reflection echo signal model and time matching filtering. It is opposite to Chapter 2's spatial processing. Waveform design primarily focuses on the Doppler-shifted time function, where a one-dimensional time signal always corresponds to a two-dimensional function of delay and Doppler. This function measures the resolution of both time delay and Doppler in the signal. The detailed derivation of the multi-target reflection echo signal model, which is the impact of target reflection on the transmitted source signal, has been conducted to prepare for subsequent promotion and simulation. Time-matched filtering, including one-dimensional sampling cross-correlation function and two-dimensional sampling cross-correlation function, leads to the expected two-dimensional convolution model of sampling cross-correlation function.
Chapter 4 is the research on the three-dimensional deconvolution filtering algorithm for azimuth - delay - Doppler, which is the core part of this paper. It includes the azimuth - delay - Doppler space - time correlation processing, MVDR multibeam system and R-L iterative deconvolution algorithm. The first section introduces the frame diagram of space - time correlation processing, laying the foundation for the process of processing in space before time. The second and third sections are arranged according to the duality of space - time, to unfold the algorithm processing part. Among them, the R-L iterative deconvolution algorithm begins with various measurement functions analysis, after briefly introducing several common deconvolution algorithms, mainly introduces the R-L algorithm used in this paper, and combines simulation analysis to detail the performance of the R-L algorithm.
Chapter 5 is the experimental verification. After detailed introduction of model and algorithm in previous chapters, this chapter is data part, which is the practical application of the above theory, divided into simulation experiments and field experiments. Simulation experiment refers to the three target's azimuth - delay - Doppler three-dimensional positioning, according to MVDR multibeam formation, followed by time delay - Doppler two-dimensional deconvolution, results show that the effectiveness of this three-dimensional space - time processing. Field experiments include both aquarium experiments and lake trials, where aquarium experiments mainly focus on distance positioning and resolution of two stationary targets, lake trials are for estimation of three-dimensional parameters of azimuth - delay - Doppler for moving targets.
Chapter 6 is the conclusion of the article, summarizing and looking forward to the overall work of the paper, and providing personal ideas for the deep study direction of the topic content.
